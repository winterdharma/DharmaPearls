  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30b12'>爾時，執樂天般遮翼子，於夜靜寂無人之時，放大光明照耆闍崛山，來至佛所。 頭面禮佛足已，在一面立。 時，般遮翼白世尊言： 「昨，梵天王至忉利天與帝釋共議。 我親從彼聞。 今者寧可向世尊說不？」</td>
    <td id='p2'>2. During the quiet of the night when there are no people, the <em>gandharva</em> Pañcaśikha illuminated Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa with his radiance as he came to visit the Buddha. After bowing his head at the Buddha’s feet, he stood to one side. Pañcaśikha then said to the Buddha, “Yesterday, the king of the Brahma Heaven went to the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven and had a conversation with Lord Śakra. I was close enough to overhear them. Would now be a good time to tell the Bhagavān what they said?”</td>

    <td class="blockquote">One time, the Trāyastriṃśa gods gathered in the Dharma Meeting Hall to hold a meeting. The four god kings were each seated in their places facing their directions. God King Dhṛtarāṣṭra sat to the east and faced west with Lord Śakra in front of him. God King Virūḍhaka sat to the south and faced north with Lord Śakra in front of him. God King Virūpākṣa sat to the west and faced east with Lord Śakra in front of him. God King Vaiśravaṇa sat to the north and faced south with Lord Śakra in front of him. Then, once the four god kings were seated, I was seated afterward.</td>

Second was the form of those gods. Third was the fame of those gods. Fourth was the pleasure of gods. Fifth was the majesty of those gods.</td>

    <td class='blockquote' id='p6'>6. After the Trāyastriṃśa gods heard these verses, they rejoiced twice as much, and they couldn’t control themselves. “The host of gods is increasing, and the host of <em>asura</em>s is dwindling!” Śakra the Lord of Gods saw the Trāyastriṃśa gods rejoicing and being merry, so he asked them, “Gentlemen, would you like to hear about the Tathāgata’s eight unequalled qualities?”</td>

    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30c16'>「諸賢，如來、至真、等正覺十號具足。 不見，過去、未來、現在，有如來、至真十號具足如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p7'>7. “Gentlemen, the Tathāgata, Arhat, and Completely Awakened One has perfected the ten epithets. I’ve not seen a Tathāgata or Arhat in the past, future, or present perfect the ten epithets like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30c18'>「佛法微妙、善可講說智者所行。 不見，過去、未來、現在，有微妙法如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p8'>8. “[Gentlemen,] the Buddha’s Dharma is a sublime and good exposition of what’s practiced by the wise. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present with a sublime teaching like that of the Buddha.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30c20'>「佛由此法而自覺悟。 通達無礙以自娯樂。 不見，過去、未來、現在，能於此法而自覺悟，通達無礙以自娯樂如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p9'>9. “[Gentlemen,] the Buddha awakened himself as a result of this teaching. He entertains himself with an understanding that has no obstacle. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present awaken themselves with this teaching and entertain themselves with an understanding that has no obstacle like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30c22'>「諸賢，佛以此法自覺悟已，亦能開示涅槃徑路，親近漸至入於寂滅。 譬如恒河水炎摩水。 二水竝流入於大海。 佛亦如是，善能開示涅槃徑路，親近漸至入于寂滅。 不見，過去、未來、現在，有能開示涅槃徑路如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p10'>10. “Gentlemen, after awakening himself with this teaching, the Buddha revealed the way to <em>nirvāṇa</em> and the gradual approach and entry into extinguishment. It’s like the Gaṅgā River and the Yamunā River. Those two rivers flow together into the ocean. The Buddha likewise reveals the way to <em>nirvāṇa</em> and the gradual approach and entry into extinguishment. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present reveal the way to <em>nirvāṇa</em> like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.30c27'>「諸賢，如來眷屬成就刹利、婆羅門、居士、沙門。 有智慧者皆是如來成就眷屬。 不見，過去、未來、現在，眷屬成就如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p11'>11. “Gentlemen, the Tathāgata has gotten a following of warriors, priests, householders, and ascetics. Wise are the followers gotten by the Tathāgata. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present get a following like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.31a1'>「諸賢，如來大眾成就，所謂比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷。 不見，過去、未來、現在，大眾成就如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p12'>12. “Gentlemen, the Tathāgata has gotten a great assembly of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present get a great assembly like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.31a3'>「諸賢，如來言、行相應。 所言如行，所行如言。 如是則為法法成就。 不見，過去、未來、現在，言、行相應，法法成就如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p13'>13. “Gentlemen, the Tathāgata’s words and actions correspond. He says what he does, and he does what he says. Thus, he has achieved one teaching and the next. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present whose words and actions correspond and who achieves one teaching and the next like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.31a6'>「諸賢，如來多所饒益，多所安樂。 以慈愍心利益天、人。 不見，過去、未來、現在，多所饒益，多所安樂如佛者也。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p14'>14. “Gentlemen, the Tathāgata has benefited many and made many happy. He compassionately bestows these blessings onto gods and people. I’ve not seen [a Tathāgata or Arhat] in the past, future, or present benefit many and make many happy like the Buddha has.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.31a9'>「諸賢，是為如來八無等法。」</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p15'>15. “Gentlemen, these are the Tathāgata’s eight unequalled qualities.”<sup id="ref2"><a href="#n2">2</a></sup></td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.31a9'>時，忉利天作是說言： 「若使世間有八佛出者，當大增益諸天眾，減損阿須倫眾！」</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p16'>16. The Trāyastriṃśa gods then said, “If eight <em>buddha</em>s were to arise in the world, the host of gods would increase a great deal, and the host of <em>asura</em>s would diminish!”</td>
  </tr>

    <td class='blockquote' id='p17'>17. The Trāyastriṃśa gods also said, “Setting aside eight <em>buddha</em>s, even if seven, six … <em>up to</em> … two <em>buddha</em>s were to arise in the world, the host of gods would increase a great deal, and the host of <em>asura</em>s would diminish! So, how would it be if it were eight <em>buddha</em>s?”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>

    <td class='blockquote' id='p18'>18. Śakra the Lord of Gods told the Trāyastriṃśa gods, “I’ve heard it from the Buddha, personally received it from him, that it never happens that two <em>buddha</em>s arise in the world at the same time. If the Tathāgata were simply to remain in the world for a long time, he would have compassion for many, would benefit many, and gods and people would find peace. Then, the host of gods would increase a great deal, and the host of <em>asura</em>s would diminish.”</td>

    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c8'>「時，大典尊復詣七百梵志所，而告之曰：『卿等當勤諷誦，廣<!-- [15] -->探道義，轉相教授，吾欲出家修無為道。所以然者？我親從梵天聞說臭穢，心甚惡之，若在家者，無由得除。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p95'>95. “‘Great Govinda then went to the 700 priests and told them, “Be diligent in your recitations, inquire into the meaning of the path, and pass on the teachings. I’m going to leave home to cultivate the unconditioned path. Why is that? I met Brahmā and heard his explanation of pollution. My heart dislikes it very much. If I remain at home, there’ll be no way to be rid of it.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c12'>時，七百梵志白典尊曰：『大師！勿出家也。夫在家安樂，五欲自娛，多人侍從，心無憂苦。出家之人獨在空<!-- [16] -->野，所欲悉無，無可貪取。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p96'>96. “‘The 700 priests said to Govinda, “Great teacher, don’t leave home! A man who lives at home can be happy and enjoy the five desires. With many people serving him, his mind has no sorrow or suffering. People who leave home live alone in the wilderness without anything that they desire and no way to get what they love.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c15'>典尊報曰：『吾若以在家為樂，出家為苦，終不出家；吾以在家為苦，出家為樂，故出家<!-- [＊] -->爾。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p97'>97. “‘Govinda replied, “If I would be happy staying at home and would suffer upon leaving home, then I would never leave home. But staying at home is suffering, and leaving home is happiness, so I will be leaving home.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c17'>梵志答曰：『大師出家，我亦出家；大師所行，我亦盡<!-- [17] -->當行。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p98'>98. “‘The priests answered, “If the great teacher leaves home, we will leave home, too. Whatever the great teacher does, we’ll all do that.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c18'>「時，大典尊至諸妻所，而告之曰：『卿等隨宜欲住者住，欲歸者歸，吾欲出家，求無為道，具論上事，明出家意。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p99'>99. “‘Great Govinda went to his wives and told them, “Do what you think is fitting. If it’s to stay here, then stay. If it’s to take refuge, then take refuge. I’m going to leave home to pursue the unconditioned path. Having discussed higher matters, the idea of leaving home became clear to me.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c21'>時，諸婦答曰：『大典尊在，一如我夫，一如我父，設今出家，亦當隨從，典尊所行，我亦宜行。』</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p100'>100. “‘His wives answered, “Great Govinda, stay here. In one way, you are like our husband, and in another way you are like our father. If you leave home now, we’ll follow you. Whatever Govinda does, we’ll do it, too.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c23'>「過七日已，時，大典尊即剃除鬚髮，服三法衣，捨家而去。時，七國王、七大居士、七百梵志及四十夫人，如是展轉，有八萬四千人同時出家，從大典尊。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p101'>101. “‘After seven days had passed, Great Govinda cut off his hair and beard, put on the three Dharma robes, renounced his home, and departed. The seven kings, seven great householders, 700 priests, and his forty wives did the same in their turn. 84,000 people left home at the same time and followed Great Govinda.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c26'>時，大典尊與諸大眾遊行諸國，廣弘道化，多所饒益。</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p102'>102. “‘Great Govinda toured the kingdoms with these great assemblies and widely educated people about the path, benefiting many.’</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.33c28'>「爾時，梵王告諸天眾曰：『時，典尊大臣豈異人乎？莫造斯觀，今釋迦文佛即其身也。世尊爾時過七日已，出家修道，將諸大眾，遊行諸國，廣弘道化，多所饒益。汝等若於我言有餘疑者，世尊今在耆闍崛山，可往問也，如佛所言，當受持之。』」</td>
    <td class='blockquote' id='p103'>103. “King Brahmā then addressed the host of gods, ‘Was this Prime Minister Govinda a different person? Don’t make this observation. Today, the Buddha Śākyamuni is his body. After seven days, the Bhagavān left home to cultivate the path, leading a great assembly to tour the kingdoms and broadly educate people about the path, benefiting many. If you have any other doubts for me, the Bhagavān is presently staying on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa. You may go and ask him. Remember well what words the Buddha says.’”</td>
  </tr>
<tr>
  <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a4'></td>
  <td class='subheading'>Teaching the Ultimate Path</td>
</tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a4'>般遮翼言：「我以是緣，故來詣此。唯然，世尊！彼大典尊即世尊是耶？世尊爾時過七日已，出家修道，與七國王乃至八萬四千人同時出家，遊行諸國，廣弘道化，多所饒益耶？」</td>
    <td id='p104'>104. Pañcaśikha said, “It was for this reason that I came here. Indeed, Bhagavān! Was that Great Govinda the Bhagavān? Did the Bhagavān leave home to cultivate the path after seven days accompanied by seven kings … 84,000 people who left home at the same time, and they toured the kingdoms broadly educating people about the path, benefiting many?”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a8'>佛告般遮翼曰：「爾時大典尊豈異人乎？莫造斯觀，即我身是也。爾時，舉國男女行來舉動，有所破損，<!-- [1] -->皆尋舉聲曰：『南無大典尊七王大相！南無大典尊七王大相！』如是至三。</td>
    <td id='p105'>105. The Buddha asked Pañcaśikha, “Was Great Govinda a different person at that time? Don’t make this observation. It was me then. At the time that I lifted up a kingdom, men and women walked towards me. Whenever they were injured, they immediately raised their voices, saying, ‘Hail, Great Govinda, the prime minister of the seven kings! Hail, Great Govinda, the prime minister of the seven kings!’ They would say this three times.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a12'>般遮翼！時，大典尊有大德力，然不能為弟子說究竟道，不能使得究竟梵行，不能使至安隱之處。其所說法，弟子受行，身壞命終，得生梵天；其次，行淺者生他化自在天；次生化自在天、兜率陀天、焰天、忉利天、四天王、剎利、婆羅門、居士大家，所欲自在。</td>
    <td id='p106'>106. “Pañcaśikha, Great Govinda possessed the power of great virtue, but he couldn’t teach the ultimate path for his disciples, couldn’t make them attain the ultimate religious life, and couldn’t make them reach the abode of peace. He taught the Dharma, and his disciples accepted and practiced it until their bodies broke up and their lives ended. They were born in the Brahma Heaven. Next, those whose practice was shallow were born in the Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven. Next were those born in the Nirmāṇarati Heaven … Tuṣita Heaven … Yama Heaven … Trāyastriṃśa Heaven … with the Four God Kings … into great families of warriors … priests … householders who could enjoy their desires freely.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a18'>「般遮翼！彼大典尊弟子，皆無<!-- [2] -->疑出家，有果報，有教<!-- [3] -->誡，然非究竟道，不能使得究竟梵行，不能使至安隱之處。其道勝者，極至梵天<!-- [＊] -->爾。</td>
    <td id='p107'>107. “Pañcaśikha, that Great Govinda’s disciples left home without doubts, and their rewards and teachings were still not the ultimate path. He couldn’t make them attain the ultimate religious life or reach the abode of peace. The best of them were born as high as the Brahma Heaven.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a21'>今我為弟子說法，則能使其得究竟道、究竟梵行、究竟安隱，終歸涅槃。我所說法弟子受行者，捨有漏成無漏，心解脫、慧解脫。於現法中，自身作證：生死已盡，梵行已立，所作已辦，更不受有。</td>
    <td id='p108'>108. “In the present, I teach the Dharma for my disciples, and I’m able to make them attain the ultimate path, ultimate religious life, and ultimate peace. They end up in <em>nirvāṇa</em>. Of those disciples to whom I teach the Dharma and who accept its practice, those who discard the contaminants and become uncontaminated are liberated at heart and liberated in wisdom. In the present life, they themselves realize: ‘Birth and death have been ended, the religious life has been established, and the task has been accomplished. I’m not subject to another existence.’</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a26'>其次，行淺者斷五下結，即於天上而般涅槃，不復還此。其次，三結盡，薄婬、怒、癡，一來世間而般涅槃；其次，斷三結，得須陀洹，不墮惡道，極七往返，必得涅槃。</td>
    <td id='p109'>109. “Next are those whose practice is shallow and end the five lower bonds. They <em>parinirvāṇa</em> in the heavens and don’t return here again. Next are those who end the three bonds and weaken lust, hate, and delusion. They’re reborn in the world once and <em>parinirvāṇa</em>. Next are those who end the three bonds and attain stream entry. They don’t fall to bad destinies and are reborn not more than seven times. They are sure to attain <em>nirvāṇa</em>.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34a29'>般遮翼！我諸弟子不<!-- [＊] -->疑出家，有果報，有教<!-- [＊] -->誡，究竟道法，究竟梵行，究竟安隱，終歸滅度。」</td>
    <td id='p110'>110. “Pañcaśikha, my disciples leave home without doubts, and they possess the rewards and teachings of the ultimate path, ultimate religious life, and ultimate peace. They end up in <em>nirvāṇa</em>.”</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td class='ch' title='t1.1.34b3'>爾時，般遮翼聞佛所說歡喜，奉行。</td>
    <td id='p111'>111. When Pañcaśikha heard what the Buddha taught, he rejoiced and approved.</td>

<h3 id="notes">Notes</h3>

<ol class="notes-list">
<li id="n1"><p>This is <em>sūtra</em> no. 3 of the <cite>Dīrgha Āgama</cite> (<a href="https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/T01n0001_p0030b10" target="_blank">T1.1.30b10-4b3</a>). The direct parallel for this <em>sūtra</em> is <a href="https://suttacentral.net/dn19" target="_blank">DN 19</a>. There’s also an longer alternate version found in the <cite>Mahāvastu</cite> and paralleled closely by a later Chinese translation (<a href="https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/T0008_001" target="_blank">Taisho No. 8</a>). [<a href="#ref1">back</a>]</p></li>
<li id="n2"><p>The three extant versions of this section of the sutra (i.e., DĀ 3, DN 19, and <cite>Mahāvastu</cite>/T8) share five items in common: The sublime Dharma, the way to <em>nirvāṇa</em>, the assembly of disciples, the matching of what the Buddha says with what he does, and his benefiting many with happiness. Each version has three other items added to make a list of eight qualities, the orders of these lists are each different from one another, and the wordings of the common items are at times quite different. We might conclude that there was originally a list of five items that has been greatly developed independently in each tradition. Generally speaking, there is more affinity between DN 19 and the <cite>Mahāvastu</cite> lists than with this list here in DĀ 3. [<a href="#ref2">back</a>]</p></li>
<li id="n3"><p><em>*Deśāmpati</em>. C. <span class="ch">地主</span>, P. <em>disampati</em>, S. <em>diśāmpati</em>. In T8, this king’s name is translated as <span class="ch">域主</span> (“regional lord”), which suggests S. <em>deśāmpati</em>. This might explain the translation here as “land lord” (<span class="ch">地主</span>), given that <span class="ch">地</span> can also mean a “region, land, or country.” The intended meaning is probably “lord of the land.” S. <em>diśāmpati</em> lit. means “direction lord,” which does not make as much sense unless it refers to the lord of one quarter of the world, as was the case for the four god kings. I’ve adopted S. *Deśāmpati here.</p>
<p><em>*Karuṇā</em>. C. <span class="ch">慈悲</span>. Both the <cite>Mahāvastu</cite> and separate Chinese translation agree with P. <em>reṇu</em>; however, the present text translates the name as “compassion.” This would suggest a name like S. <em>karuṇa</em> or <em>maitra</em>. P/S. <em>reṇu</em> on the other hand means “dust, sand, powder.” Perhaps the reading was <em>ruṇa</em>, which the C. translator took to be a contraction of <em>karuṇa</em> (?). I’ve tentatively adopted S. <em>*Karuṇā</em> here to represent the Chinese translation.</p>
<p><em>Govinda</em>. C. <span class="ch">典尊</span>, P. <em>govinda</em>, S. <em>govinda</em>. S. <em>govinda</em> lit. means “chief herdsman,” but the C. here is difficult to parse. It might be read as “chief of books,” which could certainly be a title for an administator in ancient times. It might be that the first half of the name was S. <em>grantha</em> (P. <em>gantha</em>), which can mean “book, text, verse.” To add to the uncertainty, the alternate C. version translates this name as <span class="ch">堅固</span>, which means “solid, firm, resolute” and suggests words like S. <em>dṛḍha</em>. I have kept to the traditional S. <em>govinda</em> here given the confusion in the C. translations, but it would appear that this character had different names depending on the  version of the story.</p>
<p><em>*Jyotimālā</em>. C. <span class="ch">焰鬘</span>, P. <em>jotipāla</em>, S. <em>jyotipāla</em>. The C. trans. here means “blazing garland,” which suggests S. <em>jyotimālā</em>. The alternate C. version names him <span class="ch">護明</span>, which could be read as “defender of light,” but <span class="ch">明</span> refers to clear discernment in the description that follows. We should note that in S., <em>jyotis</em> more often refers to the light of heavenly bodies than to a sacred fire. As a result, it’s often associated with astronomy, which was a discipline practiced by ancient priesthoods like the brahmins. I’ve adopted *Jyotimālā to represent the Chinese translation. [<a href="#ref3">back</a>]</p></li>
<li id="n4"><p><em>within [the boundaries of] the oceans</em>. C. <span class="ch">海內</span>. There is no P. or S. parallel for this expression, nor is there a parallel for it in the other C. translation. I read it as referring to the land mass that’s surrounded by oceans in Buddhist lore, meaning Jambudvīpa or the four continents generally, similar to the way the expression <span class="ch">天下</span> (“under heaven”) refers to the world. [<a href="#ref4">back</a>]</p></li>
<li id="n5"><p>In the other three versions, this single sentence has been expanded into a list detailing the names of the six kingdoms, the peoples occupying them, and their capital cities. In the case of DN 19 and the <cite>Mahāvastu</cite>, the expansion was accomplished by inserting verses, while T8 has an expanded prose description. [<a href="#ref5">back</a>]</p></li>
</ol>
